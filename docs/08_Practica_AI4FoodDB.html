8  Practice of Bivariate Analysis

To analyse and integrate clinical, anthropometric, and molecular data from the AI4FoodDB study using descriptive and multivariate statistical methods, including principal component analysis (PCA) and clustering, to investigate patterns of variation and associations among variables from different biological and lifestyle domains.

The AI4FoodDB study (Romero-Tapiador et al., 2023; Lacruz-Pleguezuelos et al., 2025) includes data from a nutritional intervention involving 100 participants with overweight or obesity.

The relevant datasets are:

Dataset | Data type | Examples of variables DS1 | Anthropometry | Weight, BMI, waist circumference DS2 | Lifestyle and health | Smoking, alcohol consumption, medication use DS4 | Biomarkers | Plasma proteins, glucose, cholesterol DS7 | Physical activity (IPAQ) | Minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity, step counts DS8 | Sleep (Oviedo Sleep Questionnaire) | Sleep latency, efficiency, and duration

8.1 Individual Assignments

Student | Secondary dataset | Complementary variables | Main tasks Student 1 | DS1 – Anthropometry | Four anthropometric variables | PCA and clustering incorporating protein data, plus bivariate plots Student 2 | DS2 – Lifestyle | Four lifestyle variables | PCA, clustering, and analysis of clinical associations Student 3 | DS8 – Sleep | Four Oviedo Sleep Questionnaire (OSQ) variables | PCA, clustering, and analysis of sleep–proteomics associations Student 4 | DS7 – Physical activity (IPAQ) | Four physical activity variables | PCA, clustering, and analysis of physical activity–proteomics associations

8.2 Practical Assignment Structure

Submission deadline: 15 November 2025

Each student must submit a report in Quarto Markdown (.qmd) format or an HTML report generated in RStudio. The report must include the following sections.

8.2.1 1. Data Exploration

  • Briefly describe the AI4FoodDB study and the type of data analysed.
  • Report the numbers of participants, variables, and measurement time points.
  • Provide a summary table of the selected variables (10 proteins and four variables from the complementary dataset), including measurement units, minimum and maximum values, and the number of missing observations.

8.2.2 2. Methodology and Data Sources

  • Explain how the data were collected, based on the documentation provided by AI4FoodDB.
  • Describe the instruments used, the duration of data collection where applicable, and the types of variables.
  • Justify the selection of the 10 proteins and four complementary variables.

8.2.3 3. Descriptive and Bivariate Analysis

  • Calculate descriptive statistics, including the mean, standard deviation, minimum, and maximum.
  • Produce two bivariate plots to investigate relationships between:
    1. one protein and one complementary variable; and
    2. either two proteins or two complementary variables.
  • Include a correlation plot (corrplot) for the 14 selected variables and provide a brief interpretation of the main patterns observed.

8.2.4 4. Handling Repeated Measurements

Compare the following approaches for handling the two measurements collected from each participant: (a) analyse the two measurement time points separately; or (b) calculate within-participant differences (Δ = post-intervention − pre-intervention).

Justify the selected approach and provide an example of the calculation. Discuss the implications of the choice for the subsequent analyses.

8.2.5 5. Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

  • Perform PCA using the 14 selected variables.
  • Interpret the first two principal components, identifying the main patterns of variation and associations among variables.
  • Produce a PCA biplot and interpret the results by participant groups and/or measurement time point, as appropriate.

8.2.6 6. Clustering Analysis

  • Apply either k-means clustering or hierarchical clustering.
  • Justify the selected number of clusters using appropriate criteria.
  • Present a dendrogram or a scatter plot showing cluster membership, as appropriate, and interpret the resulting groups.

8.2.7 7. Conclusions

  • Summarise and interpret the main patterns identified in the analysis.
  • Discuss potential biological or lifestyle-related hypotheses suggested by the results, while distinguishing exploratory findings from established evidence.

8.2.8 8. References

Romero-Tapiador, S., et al. (2023). “AI4FoodDB: A Database for Personalized e-Health Nutrition and Lifestyle through Wearable Devices and Artificial Intelligence.” Database, baad049.

Lacruz-Pleguezuelos, B., et al. (2025). “AI4Food, a feasibility study for the implementation of automated devices in the nutritional advice and follow up within a weight loss intervention.” Clinical Nutrition, 48, 80–89.

15.1 Review Session — 13 November

  • In-class review of each student’s progress on the .qmd report.
  • Review of the data-import and data-cleaning code.
  • Discussion of approaches to repeated measurements and variable scaling.
  • Individual feedback from the instructor.

15.2 Student Presentation Seminar — 4 December

Each student will have 15 minutes to present:

  1. A brief introduction to the study and the assigned data subset.
  2. The main findings, supported by plots, PCA, and clustering results.
  3. A biological or broader interpretative discussion.
  4. Limitations of the analysis.
